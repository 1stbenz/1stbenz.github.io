Taiwan's sudden afternoon thunderstorms frequently cause severe highway hydroplaning incidents. Many drivers focus solely on price and treadwear, overlooking the **tire performance label** that directly determines wet braking distance. Global testing protocols trace back to the UN **ECE R117 regulation**, requiring tires to meet strict **Stage 2 entry thresholds** to be sold legally.

**Starting July 1, 2026, Taiwan's BSMI officially mandates CNS 1431 energy efficiency and wet grip labeling for passenger (C1) and commercial (C2) tires!** Taiwanese consumers now have access to standardized, locally audited ratings for **Wet Grip (A–E)**, **Rolling Resistance (A–E)**, and **External Rolling Noise (A/B)**, complete with QR code lookups on physical labels.

## Lookup Tire Labels

### 1. Taiwan Official Lookup (2026 Mandatory CNS 1431)
- **Tire Energy Efficiency Information Platform (ARTC / BSMI)**: [https://tire.artc.org.tw/](https://tire.artc.org.tw/)  
  *(Search verified tire models, wet grip, rolling resistance, and noise ratings; physical labels feature a direct QR code scan)*
- **Taiwan ARTC RRC Fuel Savings Calculator**: [https://energy.artc.org.tw/Tire/rrCalculate](https://energy.artc.org.tw/Tire/rrCalculate)

### 2. International Tire Databases
- **EU Standard (EPREL Database)**: [https://eprel.ec.europa.eu/screen/product/tyres](https://eprel.ec.europa.eu/screen/product/tyres)
- **Japan Standard (Kakaku Tire Database)**: [https://kakaku.com/car_goods/tire/](https://kakaku.com/car_goods/tire/)

Before replacing your tires, always verify these ratings. As long as the **size, speed rating, and load index** are identical, specifications are globally consistent. **Avoid** tires lacking Taiwan CNS 1431 certification, those missing from EU/Japan markets, or any tire with a **Wet Grip rating below Grade A**.

Unless fuel prices are exceptionally high, there is no need to sacrifice wet safety for minor rolling resistance gains, as the fuel savings over a tire's lifespan rarely offset premium price gaps.  
**Recommended Priority**: **Wet Grip > Noise > Fuel Efficiency**.

## Wet Grip Ratings

| Wet Grip (G-value) | EU Grade | Japan Grade | Taiwan Grade (2026 CNS 1431) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1.55 ≤ G | **A** | **a** | **A** |
| 1.40 ≤ G ≤ 1.54 | **B** | **b** | **B** |
| 1.25 ≤ G ≤ 1.39 | **C** | **c** | **C** |
| 1.10 ≤ G ≤ 1.24 | **D** | **d** | **D** |
| G ≤ 1.09 | **E** | - | **E** |

> *Note: Taiwan officially enforced the A–E wet grip grading under CNS 1431 on July 1, 2026, aligned with UN ECE R117 (the previous voluntary energy tire mark did not classify wet grip).*

## Fuel Efficiency (Rolling Resistance) Ratings

| Rolling Resistance (RRC) | EU Grade | Japan Grade | Taiwan Grade (2026 New) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| RRC ≤ 6.5 | **A** | **AAA** | **A** |
| 6.6 ≤ RRC ≤ 7.7 | **B** | **AA** | **B** |
| 7.8 ≤ RRC ≤ 9.0 | **C** | **A** | **C** |
| 9.1 ≤ RRC ≤ 10.5 | **D** | **B** | **D** |
| RRC ≥ 10.6 | **E** | **C** | **E** |

> *Note: Taiwan's early voluntary mark only had Grades A, B, and C (RRC ≤ 9.0). The 2026 mandatory CNS 1431 standard aligns with EU regulations by adopting the full A–E grading scale.*

## External Rolling Noise Ratings

| Noise Grade | Taiwan CNS 1431 (2026) | EU Regulation (2020/740) | Description |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Grade A** | **≥ 3 dB(A) below limit** | **≥ 3 dB(A) below limit** | Superior quietness, lowest external noise |
| Grade B | 0 – 2 dB(A) below limit | 0 – 2 dB(A) below limit | Compliant with current statutory limits |
| Grade C | - (Not permitted) | Exceeds current limit | Phased out for new road tires |

> *Note: Physical labels also display the exact measured external noise level in **dB (decibels)**; lower numbers indicate quieter operation.*
