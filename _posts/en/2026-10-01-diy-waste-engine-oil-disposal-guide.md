### Why Is the Cable Tie (結束バンド) the True Soul of the System?

After draining, the liner holds 3 to 5 kilograms of hot, dense oil. Relying merely on "hand-tying a knot in the plastic bag" introduces severe risks:
* **Micro-Gaps from Thick Folds**: The liner material is heavy-gauge plastic. Hand knots leave microscopic creases and channel gaps. Once the bag shifts or tilts during transport, seepage is only a matter of time.
* **Reduces Usable Internal Volume**: Tying a knot consumes substantial bag length, squeezing internal absorbent material upward and potentially pushing unabsorbed residual oil toward the opening.
* **Superior Bite Force and Total Airtightness**: Using the included high-tensile nylon cable tie (結束バンド), gently purge excess air, **twist the bag neck into a tight rope shape**, and cinch the cable tie firmly at the root. Even if the box rolls over or is knocked upside down during transport, neither fumes nor oil will escape!

> **Regulatory Note (Japan vs. Other Regions)**: In Japan's municipal waste system, most local authorities permit waste oil boxes sealed with cable ties to be collected as "combustible household trash (燃やすゴミ)". **In regions such as Taiwan, however, strict municipal incinerator and air quality regulations classify waste lubricating oil as an independently recycled item.** Therefore, when using such boxes outside Japan, their primary advantages lie in **preventing workspace oil splatter** and **guaranteeing spill-free transportation**, with final disposal adhering to local recycling rules.

## Four Golden Rules of Used Oil Handling

1. **Never Pour Oil Down Drains, Sewers, or onto Soil**:
   A single liter of motor oil can contaminate nearly one million liters of clean freshwater and groundwater reserves. Violating water pollution and waste disposal laws carries severe legal penalties and substantial fines.
2. **Never Mix Different Fluids**:
   Re-refining used lubricating oil into heavy fuel oil or base stocks requires high chemical purity. Never mix **engine coolant (ethylene glycol), brake fluid (glycol ethers), gasoline, or wash water** into the waste oil container.
3. **Avoid Fragile Containers (e.g., Thin PET Water Bottles)**:
   Thin mineral water bottles (PET) have narrow openings, making pouring messy. Worse, hot oil softens and deforms thin plastic, and they easily puncture or burst under pressure in a car trunk, creating a disastrous mess.
4. **Proper Handling of Used Spin-On Oil Filters**:
   A removed metal oil filter contains residual oil. Stand it upside down and let it drain for at least 12 hours. The drained oil should be added to the waste oil bottle, while the drained steel canister can be recycled as scrap metal.
